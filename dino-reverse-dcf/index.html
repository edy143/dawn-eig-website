How to read this. The reverse DCF answers: "what revenue growth must Dino deliver to justify today's price?" If you believe Dino can grow faster than the implied figure, the shares look cheap on these assumptions; slower, and they look expensive. The forward table shows the FCFF build for your growth input.

Free cash flow to the firm = NOPAT − net reinvestment, where reinvestment = ΔRevenue ÷ sales-to-capital ratio (this ties growth to the capex Dino needs to open stores). Terminal value uses the Gordon growth model with a reinvestment rate of terminal growth ÷ terminal ROIC. Equity value = enterprise value − net debt.

Data vintage. Defaults reflect FY2024 results (revenue ≈ PLN 29.3bn, net income ≈ PLN 1.5bn, net debt ≈ PLN 196m) and a 18 Jun 2026 quote of ≈ PLN 29.46 on ≈ 980.4m shares (post 10:1 split). These are approximations for modelling — verify against primary filings before relying on any output. This is an educational tool, not investment advice.
